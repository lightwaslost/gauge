How it works

Slowdown to answer in three steps.

1

Glance

Live readings for every resource, right in the window or the menu bar.

2

Pinpoint

Helpers and background processes roll up under the app that started them, so the culprit is obvious.

3

Remember

Bookmark a spike with ⌘S, or scroll back through history to see when it keeps happening.

Features

Small app, full picture.

Six resources, one window

CPU, memory, network, disk, GPU and battery side by side, each with a live chart.

Apps, not process soup

Dozens of browser helpers become one row. Expand it when you want the detail.

Save a moment

One shortcut captures what every app was doing. Add a note and review it later.

30 days of history

A snapshot every minute, charted from the last hour to the last month, with the heaviest apps ranked.

Projects & ports

Dev servers, databases and scripts grouped by project folder, with their ports one click from the browser.

Heads-up alerts

Get a nudge when an app pins the CPU for minutes on end or quietly keeps eating memory.

Where your disk went

Folder-by-folder sizes, sorted into categories, so the 40 GB surprise is easy to find.

Battery health, too

Cycle count, capacity, temperature and live wattage, alongside charge and time left.

Private by default

Everything is recorded to a file on your Mac. Secrets in command lines are hidden on screen.

FAQ

Good to know

How do I install it?

Open the downloaded disk image and drag Gauge into your Applications folder. Launch it from there. You can turn on "Open at login" in Settings.

Which Macs are supported?

Any Mac running macOS 26 or later. GPU readings depend on what your hardware reports; if a reading isn't available, Gauge says so instead of guessing.

How much history does it keep?

One averaged snapshot per minute while Gauge is running, kept for 30 days. Nothing is recorded while your Mac is asleep or the app is quit.

Does it slow my Mac down?

Gauge samples every two seconds using the same system counters Activity Monitor reads. In the background it typically uses well under 1% of one CPU core.

Why don't I see some system processes?

macOS only lets an app read details for processes owned by your user account. Daemons that run as root aren't shown.

Is it really free?

Yes. Gauge is open source under the MIT licence — free to use for anything, including at work. There is no paid tier, no trial timer and nothing to unlock.

Why does macOS warn me on first launch?

Releases are ad-hoc signed rather than notarised with a paid Developer ID. Right-click the app and choose Open the first time, or build it from source yourself to skip the warning.

Can I contribute?

Please do — issues and pull requests are welcome on GitHub. The one hard rule is that Gauge never makes network requests, which is the whole point of it.
